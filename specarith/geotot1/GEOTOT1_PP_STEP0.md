## 1. The singular series as stated

- **[BK] (VERIFIED, eq. (4.1) p.19, (4.31)–(4.33) p.24).**
  - The local factor is ν_q(a) = |SL₂(q)|⁻¹ Σ_{ω∈SL₂(q)} e(a⟨ωe₂,e₂⟩/q), and 𝔖_𝒬(n) = Σ_{q<𝒬} |SL₂(q)|⁻¹ Σ_γ c_q(⟨γe₂,e₂⟩ − n), with c_q the Ramanujan sum.
  - Then, verbatim: "At each place, the contribution from prime powers is negligible". So (4.33) is a **lower bound** ("𝔖_𝒬(n) ≫ 𝔖(n) ≫ ∏_p(1 + |SL₂(p)|⁻¹ Σ c_p(…))"), equal to ∏_{p∤n}(1 + 1/(p²−1)) ∏_{p|n}(1 − 1/(p+1)).
  - BK therefore keep only the primes dividing n and never state an exponent-dependent factor.
  - The major arcs are only q < 𝒬 = N^{α₀/log log N} ((3.5), p.12). A factor at p ≈ √d lies outside what BK control.
- **INFERENCE: the dropped p^j terms are exactly zero here.** The Euler factor truncated at p^J equals p^J·P(d ≡ n mod p^J) under the uniform measure on SL₂(ℤ/p^J). The bottom row (c, d) is uniform over primitive vectors, of which there are p^{2J}(1 − p⁻²).
  - If p ∤ n: the factor is p²/(p²−1).
  - If p^k ∥ n, for any k ≥ 1 and any J: c must be a unit, so the factor is p^J·(p^J − p^{J−1})/(p^{2J}(1 − p⁻²)) = p/(p+1).
  - This does not depend on J, so every term with j ≥ 2 vanishes. The full 𝔖 equals (4.33) with "≍" replaced by "=", which is ζ(2)·φ(n)/n. **It depends only on the set of primes dividing n.**
- **[MOW] (VERIFIED).** Thm 1 and Thm 2 (§1) give #Γ(q)∩B_R = c_Γ R^{2δ}/#SL₂(ℤ/q) + O(q^C R^{2δ−ε}) for (q, Q₀) = 1. Thm 1.6 ("Main Theorem, elaborated") gives the coset count with γ ≡ ξ mod q; its main term R^{2δ}Ĉ/|Γ_q| does not depend on ξ.
  - MOW define no singular series and no exponent-dependent factor.
  - Their Appendix (Bourgain–Kontorovich–Magee, Thm 2) removes the square-free restriction on q. That is the only place prime powers appear.
  - The term q^C makes all of this vacuous when q ≈ R.
- **[Hen94] (VERIFIED: abstract, Thm 1 p.47, Cor. 1 p.48, (27)–(30) p.53).** Equidistribution holds over the whole group G_n of det ±1 matrices mod n, for **every** n and m ≥ 2. The counting factors (28)–(30) are products over p | gcd(d, n), i.e. over primes only, so the frequency of d ≡ b (mod n) does not depend on the exponent.
  - This is a theorem for fixed n with the count summed over d < x. It says nothing about a single fixed q.

## 2. Strong approximation for {1..N}, N = 3, 4, 5

- **VERIFIED**, [BK] (3.2) p.12: "for all q ≥ 1, the reduction of Γ is full, Γ(mod q) ≅ SL₂(q), which is anyway the case for any alphabet A containing 1 and 2". Remark 1.31 (p.6) says the same: B = 1 for {1,2}.
- **VERIFIED**, [Hen94] p.46: the generated semigroup reduces onto the full det ±1 group G_n for every n.
- **INFERENCE (proof).**
  1. The image mod q of a semigroup is a finite semigroup inside a finite group, so it is a group.
  2. M₁⁻¹M₂ = [[1,1],[0,1]] and M₂M₁⁻¹ = [[1,0],[1,1]], so the group generated by Γ_{1,2} is SL₂(ℤ).
  3. Γ_{1,2} ⊂ Γ_{1..N}, so Γ_{1..N} mod q = SL₂(ℤ/q) for all q.
  4. With the det −1 generators, G_{1..N} mod q is all of {det ±1}.
- **[check]** BFS closures give the full group size mod 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 3, 9, 27, 81, 5, 25, 125, 7, 49, 6, 12, 36 and 72.
- **There are no exceptional primes: p = 2 and 3 lift to every p^k.**
- **[check]** Canonical enumeration to Q = 10⁵ (N = 3) and Q = 4·10⁴ (N = 4) shows K = c·(q/φ)/q^{2δ−1} independent of v₂(q), v₃(q), v₅(q), v₇(q) to within about 0.5% (≤ 2σ). Examples:
  - N = 4, p = 2, v = 0..5+: 1.3955, 1.3928, 1.3999, 1.3911, 1.3901, 1.3886, each ±0.001–0.005.
  - The overall means, 1.404 (N = 3) and 1.395 (N = 4), reproduce your constants.

## 3. Predicted excess for p^k, and what the derivation misses

- **The local-factor prediction (INFERENCE, §1): the excess is exactly 0 for every p and k.** This includes q = 2^k, 3^k, 5^k and 7^k. The factor is (1 − 1/p) regardless of exponent, and the relative density is (p−1)/p.
- **For p ≥ 317 no local-factor error could explain your result anyway.** The entire p-adic factor differs from 1 by at most 1/p ≤ 0.3%. The observed +0.012 is about 0.9%, roughly 6× the whole q/φ(q) correction for p².
- **The missing piece: the folding lemma, which is global, not local.**
  - **SECONDARY (Niederreiter 1986, as printed in [Mo] §5.2 p.24):** ⟨b₁..b_s, X, 1, c₁−1, c₂..c_s⟩ = (X+1)·r², where r = ⟨b₁..b_s⟩ and c = the reversed word.
  - **INFERENCE:** setting X = 0 and merging the zero partial quotient gives
    a/r = [b₁..b_s] ⇒ [b₁,…,b_{s−1}, b_s+1, b_s−1, b_{s−1},…,b₁] has denominator exactly r².
    **[check]** The identity holds for all 194,064 cases tested (r < 400, X = 0..3).
  - Consequence: every good a/r with b_s ≤ N−1 yields a good fraction with denominator r², so c(r²) ≥ F_N(r) ≈ const·r^{2δ−1}. This is a deterministic, near-palindromic subpopulation with relative size about q^{−(2δ−1)/2} (2–6% at q ≈ 4·10⁵).
  - **INFERENCE (general form):** the word w·v·w^R has denominator equal to the quadratic form Sym(M(v)) evaluated at (q_{s−1}, q_s). Degenerate forms m·ℓ² give these families for any q = m·r² with m ≤ N+1. **They apply to all squares and to m·r², whether or not q is a prime power. They do not apply to p³, p⁵ or 7⁷.**
- **[check] 103 p², p ∈ [317, 997], against the mean of the 4 nearest primes.**

  | N | excess in K | folded lower bound F | difference |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3 | 0.051 ± 0.017 | 0.059 | −0.008 ± 0.017 |
  | 4 | 0.007 ± 0.006 | 0.026 | −0.019 ± 0.006 |
  | 5 | 0.011 ± 0.003 | 0.016 | −0.005 ± 0.003 |

  - Controls with no fold available, which are null:
    - p³ (11 values): N = 4 −0.008 ± 0.019; N = 5 +0.006 ± 0.012.
    - r² − 1 (73 values): N = 4 −0.009 ± 0.011.
  - **Composite squares have a much larger excess** (145 values: N = 3/4/5 = 0.39 / 0.12 / 0.055). Even r² carry most of it (N = 4: 0.18 ± 0.03), plausibly because 4k² admits extra (m = 4) families.
  - A near-palindrome census (outer palindromic match ≥ L/2 − 4, either expansion) accounts for the excess in each group: residuals −0.04 to +0.03, all within 1.5σ.
  - **Verdict.** The prime-power excess is the folding / near-palindrome effect, not a local factor. It is not specific to prime powers.
  - **N = 4 is open.** The net excess there is 3σ below the folded count, so some offsetting deficit exists. One candidate (INFERENCE, proven): for q = p², **no convergent denominator of any a/q is divisible by p**, since gcd(q_j, q) ≤ p(tail) < q/q_j. Its size is unquantified.
  - The squares inside your "smooth" and "other" classes carry the same excess, but they are too rare (about 0.1%) to move class means.
- **Recommended re-cut of your tables:**
  - Split the prime powers into fold-eligible (q = m·r², m ≤ N+1) and not eligible.
  - Use matched-size prime controls.
  - Regress the residual on F_N(√q)·(q/φ)/q^{2δ−1}.

## 4. Fixed-q numerics in the literature

- **[KK] (VERIFIED, p.1–2, English summary).** These are lower bounds for f(a^m, N) = #{sequences with entries < N and continuant a^m}, e.g. f ≥ C₁(s)·m^{log₂ λ}. They are polylogarithmic in q, with no asymptotics or tables, and the method is folding.
- **[Mo] §5.2 (VERIFIED p.23–24).** Folding results for 2^α and 3^α (k = 4), 5^α (k = 5), 6 and 7^r2^r; Kan–Krotkova; Korobov's thesis. No per-q counts.
- **OEIS [check].** Canonical c₂(n) and c₃(n) for n = 2..25 match no sequence. Only the minimum-k and max-quotient entries exist (A141821, A141822).
- **Kan 2019 (Sb. Math. 210(3), abstract only).** About the alphabet {1,2,3,5} and the count of denominators ≫ N^{0.85}. Not per-q.
- **I found no published numerical study or conjecture for the fixed-q count c(q), for prime powers or otherwise. The square / folding excess appears unpublished as a count statement.**

## 5. The second-order term of the mean depth

- **No source I read states a fixed-q or prime-q mean of Σa_i with an explicit ln q coefficient.**
  - [ABH] Thm 5 (p.~6) gives counts L_{[b,c]} with error O((log log N)³/b), too weak to give it.
  - Panov and Liehl give only "~" (SECONDARY via [ABH] eq. (1)).
  - The Yao–Knuth abstract has error O(n log n (log log n)²).
  - Avdeeva–Bykovskii (Dokl. 2013, fixed denominator) is UNVERIFIED.
- **Closed form (INFERENCE).** Your Farey conversion simplifies to
  **β = (12/π²)(2γ − 1 − 2ζ′(2)/ζ(2)) = 1.57375.**
- **[check] Prime q from 10³ to 3·10⁶ (12 primes per point).**
  - Mean S − (6/π²)ln²q − 1.574 ln q runs −0.491, −0.506, −0.521, −0.525, −0.526, −0.528, −0.528, −0.528.
  - It is flat from 10⁵ on: the drift is under 0.002 over Δln q = 3.4, so β_prime = 1.5734 ± 0.001.
  - That is S = Σa_i. Your −1.53 is the same thing with depth = S − 1.
  - So β for primes agrees with the Farey-derived value. Any arithmetic ln q term (such as Σ_{p|q} ln p/(p−1)) is invisible for primes. The O(1) constant, about −0.528, has no source.
