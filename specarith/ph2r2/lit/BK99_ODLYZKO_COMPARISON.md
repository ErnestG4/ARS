**What was compared with data (Odlyzko's zeros, refs. [39, 40] there), visually, through the number variance Σ²(x):**
- **Fig. 3** (p. 249): Σ²(x) near n = 10¹² (t ≈ 2.677·10¹¹, ⟨d⟩ = 3.895), theory from the diagonal form factor (4.18)
  with τ* = 1/4, against the zeros. Text: "it reproduces almost perfectly this statistic as computed from numerical
  values of high zeros [39, 40]. Figure 3 shows that the agreement extends from the random-matrix regime (small x) to
  the far nonuniversal regime. Note however the tiny oscillatory deviations". Caption: "the theory fails to capture
  small, fast oscillations in the data."
- **Fig. 6** (p. 252): Σ²(x) near n = 10⁹ (t ≈ 3.719·10⁸, ⟨d⟩ = 2.848), theory "(4.19) and (4.26), including the
  off-diagonal correction (4.27)–(4.28) [70]" — i.e. the full Bogomolny–Keating pair correlation — against the zeros
  (dots); the correction "accurately reproduces the oscillatory deviation (Figure 3)".
- **The pair correlation R(x) itself** (Fig. 5, eq. 4.19 + 4.23, zeros near n = 10⁵) is shown from theory only, against
  R_GUE — no data overlay. Fig. 4 shows the nonuniversal correction R_c alone (theory).
- Formula attribution there: R_c "was obtained for both the Riemann zeros and for general systems in [41], and
  independently in [42] for the Riemann zeros" (Bogomolny–Keating; reference list not transcribed here).

**Consequence for the R₂ phase (Will, R2).** The BK/CS two-point function with its arithmetic lower-order terms has been
compared with Odlyzko's zeros before — visually, through Σ², at t ≈ 3.7·10⁸ and 2.7·10¹¹, with near-perfect agreement.
The R₂ phase is therefore framed as an **independent quantitative replication**: the new elements are a calibrated
amplitude μ of the lower-order term with a CI, across five fresh heights (10⁶–2·10¹⁰; R3 at t ≈ 4.1·10⁸ sits next to BK's
Fig. 6 height), with sealed nulls and red paths.
