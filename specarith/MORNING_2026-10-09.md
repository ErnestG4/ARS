**Finding for A1: R1's bootstrap SD doubles at 10,000-level blocks, and this is an instrument effect, not structure.** The surrogate's blocks are independent, so it has no long-range correlation. The cause is that the mean density drifts across R1's height range (t from 6.7 M to 8.8 M), and the test function's per-level expected contribution E[c](t) drifts with it, by +3.7%. A moving-block bootstrap counts a deterministic trend as variability. Its extra variance is n·B·D²/12, which grows with block length B. That predicts a ratio of 2.11 at B = 10,000, against 2.03 observed; for (2.0, 0.3), the prediction is 2.02 against 2.07 observed. R2–R5 drift by less than 0.5%, the predicted effect is under 4%, and their ratios are flat. Generator: `ph2r2/g0b_trend_check.py` → `results/g0b_trend_check.txt`.
- The real zeros have the same drift. **If A1's growth is read raw, R1 will "grow" by about 2× at 10,000 whatever the zeros do.** That growth is the drift, not long-range arithmetic structure.
- My proposal, for your decision; A1 is descriptive, so no seal-text change is forced. Report A1 three ways:
  - (i) raw;
  - (ii) as the real ratio divided by this per-bin surrogate ratio (the drift baseline);
  - (iii) on residuals c_i − E[c](t_i), which remove the drift exactly.
  Only growth beyond (ii)/(iii) would count as the zeros' long-range structure.
- The drift also makes R1's sealed CI conservative, at about 2× the surrogate SD. Power is still 1.000.
