## A. Warmup → Arm B Q1 (row 14; §3 LR confound)

1. **Kalra & Barkeshli, arXiv 2406.09405 (NeurIPS 2024).** Warmup lets training tolerate a larger target LR by pushing it to lower λ_max; two regimes: *progressive sharpening* (small init/μP, **normalised transformers**: rising LR meets rising sharpness → a chain of catapults) vs *sharpness reduction* (SP). For Adam the preconditioned sharpness starts at 10⁵–10⁶ (v₀ ≈ 0) and warmup lets it decay. Pre-LN LM, WikiText-103. Post-warmup trajectories differ little **at the loss level**. | empirical, small | row 14, §3 | **SUGGESTS NEW TEST**: Pre-LN 70M is in the sharpening regime; the ~2k turning points may anchor to "preconditioned sharpness first hits its threshold", outside the sealed {STEP, WARMUP, LR_INT}. | **y** — a fourth, curvature-defined time map as the next Q1 pre-reg (HVPs on banked 70M checkpoints are CPU-cheap).

2. **[v1] Kosson et al., arXiv 2410.23922.** New use only: the per-step angular update ‖ΔW‖/‖W‖ per matrix is a second candidate Q1 anchor variable beside item 1's. | n (folds into 1).

3. **Alimisis, Islamov & Lucchi, arXiv 2510.03164** (theory: curvature linear in suboptimality makes the optimal LR rule a warmup); **Phunyaphibarn et al., arXiv 2311.15051** (momentum GD at large LR has *large catapults*); **[classic] Gilmer et al., arXiv 2110.04369** (λ_max is dynamic and coupled to the schedule); **Wortsman et al., arXiv 2309.14322** (attention-logit growth at high LR; longer warmup/qk-LN flatten LR sensitivity). | theory / empirical | §3, lead 6 | CONFIRM warmup is a curvature response and warmup length changes the trajectory's shape, not its timing (consistent with NO SIMPLE ANCHOR). | n.

## C. Adam/Muon at the edge of stability → pdyn attribution (PDYN_FINDINGS §4–6)

6. **[classic] Cohen et al., arXiv 2207.14484.** Large-batch Adam equilibrates *preconditioned* sharpness at 38/η (β₁ = 0.9) while raw sharpness grows. | CONFIRMS the stability variable is preconditioned curvature. | n.

7. **Cohen, Damian, Talwalkar, Kolter & Lee, "Central flows", arXiv 2410.24206 (ICLR 2025).** Time-averaged EoS trajectories of GD/RMSProp obey a derivable ODE that predicts long horizons; adaptation acts on the β₂ scale. | theory + empirical | PDYN §4 cand. 2 | CONFIRMS a short-memory velocity model with optimiser time scales as the right C1 class. | n.

8. **Fong & Yang, arXiv 2608.20638.** Adam's second moment is negative feedback on *active curvature* (preconditioned curvature along the gradient); at EoS consecutive gradients are nearly antiparallel (**gradient reversal**), incl. **GPT-2 medium**. | empirical + theory | PDYN §6 (τ_v 5–10; kurtosis residual) | **SUGGESTS NEW TEST**: reversal makes the update autocorrelation negative at lag 1 along top-curvature directions and positive (momentum) elsewhere, so τ_v should differ between spike and bulk bands: compute C(x) for top-32 vs bulk separately on phase-1 data (CPU); the M4 hook measures the update autocorrelation directly. | **y** for A1: fix τ_v from the measured update autocorrelation, per band, not from β₁ alone.

9. **Bai et al., arXiv 2506.04805 (ICML 2026)** — Adam spikes arise when v_t *decouples* from g_t², holding preconditioned λ_max above 2/η (1/(1−β₂) ≈ 20-step scale); **Regis & Chewi, "Rod flow", arXiv 2605.06821** — β₁ and β₂ are distinct time scales in an EoS-faithful continuous model of Adam/NAdam/RMSProp; **Andreyev & Beneventano, arXiv 2412.20553** — under minibatch SGD what sits at 2/η is *batch sharpness* (> λ_max). | theory + empirical | lead 6, A1, item 1 | CONFIRM two momentum time scales; any curvature anchor at Arm B's batch size is the stochastic one. | n.

10. **[v1] Olsen et al., arXiv 2507.12709.** New reading only (HTML checked today): the SDE is SGD-only with white noise and no weight decay, and repulsion is never tested by spacing or velocity statistics. Its white-velocity limit is exactly the τ_v → 0 DBM that reproduced 40/48 in PDYN §6. | PDYN P2 | pdyn is its first spacing/velocity test, under Adam/Muon. | n, but PDYN_PREREG should cite it and name these assumptions as what A1 corrects.

## D. Hessian structure, GN vs full → Arm A (ARMA §2–3), rows 10/11/16

11. **Dauphin, Agarwala & Mobahi, arXiv 2401.10809 (NeurIPS 2024 per mlanthology).** Hessian = Gauss-Newton (feature exploitation) + Nonlinear Modelling Error (exploration); NME vanishes at interpolation but is large during feature learning. | theory + empirical | ARMA §3 | Report 3 = 1 × 2 (input projection × output curvature) **only for the GN part**; LMs never interpolate, so an NME residual, possibly negative, is expected. **SUGGESTS**: bank (3 − 1×2) per band as the NME share; allow sign-negative antithetic costs in the regime check. | **y** — one declared quantity before sealing.

12. **Meterez, Nair, Morwani, Pehlevan, Kakade & Damian, "A Defense of the Quadratic Model", arXiv 2607.21716.** 150M OLMo-style LM, 3B tokens, Adam, Lanczos: head/tail split at index ≈ vocab (unembedding-dominated head); tail exponent ≈0.96 (GN) vs ≈0.65 (full H); GN and H agree in the head, disagree in the tail; negative eigenvalues localised to mlp.up; tail universal across batch 1/64/1024 and preconditioner; local quadratics predict 5–10% of training; training sits within 2× of a batch-size-dependent edge of stability. | empirical, 150M | ARMA §2–3, row 9 | **CONFIRMS** arm A's bulk bands live in the tail where GN ≠ H; expect negative curvature in MLP_IN bands. | y (same amendment as 11).

13. **Ormaniec, Dangel & Singh, arXiv 2410.10986 (ICLR 2025 spotlight per listing)** — exact self-attention Hessian: Q/K blocks depend on higher data/attention moments than V; GN vs functional parts weigh differently per block; **Petrov et al., arXiv 2510.16927** — closed-form full-block Hessian validated against autodiff. | theory | ARMA §3, §6 | CONFIRM per-type curvature anisotropy is architectural: normalise per matrix type (the skeleton does) and do **not** pool across types in the hierarchical fit. | n (make explicit).

SUGGESTS: compare per-type stable-rank fall timing with per-type sharpness emergence on Arm B. | n.

15. **Zhang et al., "Why Transformers Need Adam", arXiv 2402.16788 (NeurIPS 2024).** SLQ blockwise spectra: block heterogeneity (JS 286 ViT vs 0.1 ResNet) is present **at init** and *reduces* over pre-training; GPT-2 125M, ViT, BERT. | empirical | row 16 | CONFIRMS Muon-vs-AdamW differences should be largest early (Q4's "early delay"). | n.

16. **Song, Ahn & Yun, arXiv 2405.16002 (ICLR 2025).** Gradient alignment with the top Hessian eigenspace is spurious: projecting SGD onto it stops loss decrease; projecting *away* works; holds at EoS, with momentum/adaptive optimisers. | empirical | rows 10/15, H_RES | CONFIRMS learning progress lives outside the curvature spike — in Hessian coordinates; W's singular basis differs (commensurability check before mapping). | n.

17. **Deng et al., "Depth, Not Data", arXiv 2602.00545**; **Dong et al., arXiv 2505.02809.** Hessian bulk/spike bifurcation arises in deep linear nets with balanced data (gap ∝ depth); near-block-diagonality follows from class count C at init. | theory | row 9 | CONFIRM caution: Hessian outliers and blocks are partly architectural. | n.

## Synthesis
- **Q1 (row 14):** the literature's anchor for early transitions is curvature (preconditioned/batch sharpness reaching threshold; angular update size), not step, warmup end or LR integral; LR_INT as least-bad fits a sharpness-integrated clock. Pre-register a fourth map.
- **A0r floor:** divergence is seeded in the first ~0.5% of training and is non-linear; fork-step perturbation runs give c_pair with n > 1 cheaply and test warmup-length dependence (A2's heavier velocity kurtosis is the same window).
- **pdyn A1:** cite 2507.12709 as the DBM prior; fix τ_v from the measured update autocorrelation (M4) with two time scales (β₁, β₂), allowed to differ between spike and bulk bands.
- **Arm A §3:** declare the NME residual (3 − 1×2), permit negative antithetic costs, pool only within matrix type.
